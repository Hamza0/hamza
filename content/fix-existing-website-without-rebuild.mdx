If your website has stopped doing something important, you may not need a new one. I would first find out what is broken, whether the rest of the site still works, and whether the problem can be repaired safely.

## Fix the part that is costing you business

Imagine you run a small cleaning company. Your website looks fine, people find it on Google, but quote requests have stopped reaching your inbox. I would not start by redesigning your homepage. I would send a test enquiry and follow it to the receiving inbox.

Even if a form looks fine, I would check whether it actually submits and whether the receiving system accepts the message. A failure in spam verification or email delivery calls for a focused repair, not a new website.

## When a repair makes sense

I would keep your current website if its platform still receives updates, you can access its accounts, and the problem is limited to a few features. That might mean a broken contact form, checkout, mobile layout or a connection to another tool.

A rebuild becomes worth discussing if the software is no longer supported, repeated repairs keep breaking other features, or the existing structure cannot support the work your business now needs.

## What I need to see first

Send me the public website address and describe the problem in your own words. For example, "Customers fill in the quote form but we never receive anything." Mention when you first noticed it.

I can start with what is visible publicly. If further investigation needs access, we can agree on that separately. Keep your passwords and customer information private.

I would rather identify a small repair that works than recommend a replacement without understanding the problem. If you do decide to rebuild later, there is also the question of [keeping your existing Google visibility](/blog/website-redesign-google-traffic-drop).
