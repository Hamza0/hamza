If your form says “sent” but your inbox stays empty, I would trace the enquiry from submission to delivery. The confirmation alone cannot explain what happened.

## Check what “sent” means

WordPress explains that its [email sending function](https://developer.wordpress.org/reference/functions/wp_mail/) can report success without confirming receipt. The form's wording may describe only one part of the process.

An email-connection test, often called an SMTP test, also differs from submitting the actual form. It does not check every recipient address or notification rule. WPForms explains this in its [SMTP troubleshooting guidance](https://wpforms.com/docs/how-to-set-up-smtp-using-the-wp-mail-smtp-plugin/).

If your Submit button produces an error or never finishes, mention that. It points to a different investigation from a completed submission with a missing email.

## Follow the enquiry

| Check | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Form entry, if stored | The submitted details and time |
| Notification settings | The recipient and conditions for sending |
| Email service | Whether it accepted or rejected the message |
| Receiving inbox | The message, including in spam |

WPForms' [notification guidance](https://wpforms.com/docs/troubleshooting-email-notifications/) covers disabled notifications, incorrect addresses and rules that depend on someone's answers.

A working mail connection does not guarantee inbox delivery. I would follow one affected message before changing several settings and losing track of what helped.

## Keep useful details

Keep your page address, submission time and time zone, exact confirmation and intended recipient together. Note whether every enquiry is missing or only those with certain answers.

If you ask for help, include your form plugin and email integration versions. Mention recent website, hosting or email changes without assuming they caused the problem.

Preserve saved entries if the form stores them. Some forms do not retain a copy. Remove unnecessary customer details from screenshots or logs you share.

## Test the actual form

Send a clearly labelled test through your public form using non-sensitive details. Follow it through the confirmation, any saved entry, email service and receiving inbox. If different answers send enquiries to different people, check those routes too.

Record what changed and what you confirmed. The [contact experience on the page](/blog/a-fast-website-still-needs-a-clear-next-step) and the email arriving both deserve a check.
