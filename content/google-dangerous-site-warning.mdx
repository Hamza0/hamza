A red browser warning about your website is different from a slow page or an ordinary certificate error. If Chrome or Google flags the website as dangerous, I would treat it as a possible security incident until the cause is understood.

Do not ask your customers to ignore the warning or continue entering personal information.

## Check what Google is reporting

If you own the website, open its Security Issues report in Google Search Console. The report may show affected pages or types of harmful content. [Google's guide to dangerous site warnings](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/6347750?hl=en) explains what owners should investigate.

I would also check with the hosting provider, review recent changes and look for unfamiliar redirects or files using safe administrative access. An unexpected advert or redirect may come from a compromised plugin, injected content or another connected component.

## Protect information before restoring the website

Imagine an online shop receives a report that customers are being redirected to an unfamiliar login page. Removing one visible redirect is not enough to establish that the website is safe.

I would preserve relevant logs and backups, identify the affected systems, and assess whether access credentials or customer information might have been exposed. The hosting provider or a qualified incident response specialist may need to help. If customer information may be involved, seek appropriate privacy and legal guidance promptly.

## A warning needs a proper review

Once the underlying issue has been resolved and the website has been checked, an owner can follow Google's review process where applicable. [Google's security documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/security/social-engineering) explains its approach to deceptive content. A review does not replace the actual cleanup, and I would not promise an immediate removal of a warning.

If your browser shows only [Your connection is not private](/blog/website-connection-not-private), that could be a certificate issue instead.

Tell me the public website address and the warning you see. Do not send passwords, private logs or customer records in the enquiry. If the website is accepting payments or personal information, mention that so I can understand what is at risk.
