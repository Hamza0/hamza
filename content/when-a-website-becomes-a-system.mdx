A website becomes part of a business system when an action starts work elsewhere, such as assigning an enquiry, retrieving information or sending a notification.

## Start with the business job

Some websites need to explain an organisation, show its services and help people make contact. Clear pages, accessible navigation and dependable publishing may meet that need.

Other websites take on work. A form might assign an enquiry to a team, while a dashboard brings in regularly updated data. Those connections need to be planned and checked alongside the visible pages.

The design can stay simple. The extra responsibility is making the whole process work, including when another service is unavailable.

## Follow one enquiry from start to finish

Imagine a visitor completing a contact form. The website checks the details, another service accepts the request and someone follows up.

For that journey, establish:

- What information is required, and where the main record is stored.
- Who handles the next step and how they receive the request.
- What the visitor sees if a connection fails, and how the team investigates.

A tidy form can still send the wrong fields. Following the full journey finds problems that a visual review would miss.

[Missing API replies and retries](/blog/apis-are-where-systems-start-talking) explain one example: an error on screen does not always mean the underlying work failed.

## Make ownership and information clear

Someone should know who can change an integration's settings, which system holds the current record and where to investigate failures. Document how information moves between systems, especially when they use different formats.

Content needs an owner too. The editing process should work for the person publishing information each week.

My workforce data experience also taught me to consider the path into a tool. Labels, sources and explanations help people understand a chart or planning screen. That context belongs in [data tools for non-technical teams](/blog/building-data-tools-for-non-technical-teams), alongside accessible controls and useful messages when no results are available.

## Choose the smallest useful scope

Write down the outcome before adding another integration. If clear pages and a dependable form meet the need, keep the project focused.

If the task crosses several systems, include those connections, their owners and their failure paths. Before launch, follow the journey with realistic content and leave enough information for the team maintaining it.
