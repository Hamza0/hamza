Your form can look finished while the enquiry goes nowhere. I follow what happens after each action, so the website supports the work you need done.

## Start with what you need it to do

If you need to explain your business, show services and help people contact you, clear pages and dependable publishing may be enough.

If your form assigns work to a team or your dashboard brings in fresh data, the project has more moving parts. I would include those connections in the plan alongside the visible pages.

The design can stay simple. The extra work is making the full process dependable, including when another service is unavailable.

## Follow one enquiry together

Imagine someone completing your contact form. The website checks their details, another service accepts the request and your team follows up.

I would ask:

- What information do you need, and where is the main record stored?
- Who handles the next step, and how do they receive the request?
- What does the visitor see if something fails, and how will your team investigate?

A tidy form can still send the wrong fields. Following the whole journey reveals problems you cannot spot by looking at the page.

[Missing API replies and retries](/blog/apis-are-where-systems-start-talking) show one example: an error on screen does not always mean the underlying work failed.

## Make it clear who looks after what

You should know who can change the connection's settings, which system holds the current information and where to investigate failures. I want the way information moves between systems to be understandable, especially when their formats differ.

Content needs an owner too. If you're publishing every week, the editing process should fit that job.

My workforce data experience also taught me to consider how you find and understand a tool. Labels, sources and explanations help you interpret a chart or planning screen. That context belongs in [data tools for non-technical teams](/blog/building-data-tools-for-non-technical-teams), along with accessible controls and useful messages when no results are available.

## Build the smallest thing that does the job

I would write down your intended outcome before adding another integration. If clear pages and a dependable form meet the need, keep the scope there.

If the task crosses several systems, include their connections, owners and failure paths. Before launch, walk through the journey with realistic content and leave the team enough information to maintain it.
