Choose a regular task with clear rules. I would start small enough that your team can check the result and recover when something goes wrong.

## Follow one real example

Take one of your website enquiries and follow it until someone responds. Who handles it? Where do they copy information, switch systems or wait for someone else?

Include awkward cases: a missing phone number, a duplicate message or a request sent to the wrong team. These help reveal which parts need judgement.

Microsoft's [process analysis guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-automate/process-advisor-overview) describes mapping work to find delays and automation opportunities. A small team can start with a shared document and a few examples.

## Separate routine work from decisions

For your team, a useful starting point might be one of these:

| Task | Part to automate | Keep a person involved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enquiries | Create a record and assign it | Decide whether the job is a good fit |
| Invoices | Collect documents and flag missing details | Approve unusual charges |
| Reports | Gather agreed figures | Explain unexpected changes |

Microsoft's [automation recommendations](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/well-architected/operational-excellence/automate-tasks) favour clearly defined tasks and allow human decisions within a partly automated process.

## Measure the current effort

Before building anything, I would measure how often the task happens, how long it takes and how much correction it needs. Keep waiting time separate: a five-minute task might sit in your inbox for two days.

For example, twenty records taking four minutes each represent eighty minutes of manual entry. The eventual saving will also depend on time spent reviewing exceptions and maintaining the automation.

Choose a goal your team can check, such as entering the same details fewer times while still assigning every enquiry to someone.

## Decide who handles problems

Decide who on your team receives failure alerts and knows how to resume unfinished work. Check what happens when a request arrives twice or a connection fails.

[A timed-out request may already have completed](/blog/apis-are-where-systems-start-talking), so retrying needs care. Try one small part of the process first and review its exceptions before expanding it.
