A redesign can change the pages Google already knows, even when the new website looks better. If traffic falls after launch, I would compare the old and new URLs, check redirects and identify which pages actually lost search visits.

## Start with the pages that lost visits

Use Search Console to compare clicks and impressions before and after launch, then look at individual pages and searches. Google's [traffic-drop guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/debugging-search-traffic-drops) recommends examining the pattern instead of assuming one cause.

Did every page decline, or only services whose addresses changed? Did impressions fall too, or mainly clicks? Keep the launch date beside that comparison.

The timing makes the redesign worth investigating. It does not rule out changing demand, ranking changes or another technical issue.

## Match each old page to its real replacement

Imagine `/services/roof-repair` becomes `/roofing/repairs`. Someone following the old address should reach the relevant replacement, with the service information still available.

I would create a simple mapping of old URL, new URL and redirect status. A permanent redirect tells browsers and search engines that a page has moved. Google's [site-move guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/site-move-with-url-changes) recommends server-side permanent redirects to the corresponding new pages.

Sending every retired page to the homepage can confuse visitors and may be treated as a missing-page error. Where several articles were genuinely combined, the consolidated article can be the appropriate destination.

## Check what the launch carried over

A useful migration check includes:

- Important service details, headings and links that existed before launch.
- Canonical tags, which identify the preferred page address, pointing to the intended URLs.
- Any temporary `noindex` instruction removed from pages that should appear in search.
- Navigation and sitemap entries using the new addresses.

Google needs to recrawl and process URL changes, so fluctuations can happen. A fixed recovery date would be a guess. Monitor the affected pages and their replacements together.

If the redesign also changed hosting or security, check [whether your CDN is blocking Google](/blog/cdn-googlebot-crawl-problems). For page copy that needs rebuilding, start with [clear answers to real customer questions](/blog/seo-and-aeo-start-with-clear-answers).
