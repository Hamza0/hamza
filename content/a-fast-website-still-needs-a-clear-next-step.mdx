---
title: "Why Aren’t Website Visitors Contacting You?"
publishedAt: "2026-11-24"
updatedAt: "2026-11-24"
summary: "Check your wording, buttons and forms so visitors can contact you easily."
---

If people visit your website but rarely contact you, I would check your offer, button labels and form before assuming you need more traffic.

## Explain what you offer

Read your page as a first-time visitor. Is it clear what you do, where you work and which problems you can help with?

Google's [Core Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals) measure loading, responsiveness and unexpected movement on a page. Those checks are useful, but they cannot tell you whether your service description makes sense.

Put the information customers need near the contact option: services, location, relevant examples and what happens after they get in touch.

## Give buttons clear names

Give your visitor a clear idea of what will happen when they click. “Request a quote” should open a quote request, with the questions they need to answer.

| What the visitor wants | Clear link wording |
| --- | --- |
| See your experience | View a project |
| Discuss a broken website | Request a website review |
| Arrange a call | Book a consultation |

W3C's [link guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/link-purpose-in-context.html) explains why people need to understand a link's purpose from its wording or surrounding text.

## Keep the form manageable

Ask for information you will use. A website address, email and short description may be enough for an initial review.

Give every field a label. Explain errors beside the affected field and preserve answers when someone goes back. For a longer form, show progress and make required questions clear without relying on colour alone.

Try it on a phone and with a keyboard. Check that the form remains understandable when an answer is missing or incorrect.

## Check where enquiries go

I would then send a labelled test through your form and follow it into the intended inbox or customer record. A success message on the page does not confirm that everything finished.

If a connection fails, show an accurate message and preserve recoverable answers. [Repeating an uncertain request can create duplicates](/blog/apis-are-where-systems-start-talking), so someone needs to check how retries work.

Compare your visits, form starts and completed enquiries. You can then focus on where people get stuck instead of guessing what to redesign.
