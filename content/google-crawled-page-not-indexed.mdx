Google fetched the URL but has not included it in its search index. That status alone does not identify the cause. I would first check whether it affects an important page, then inspect what Google actually received.

Google's [Page indexing guidance](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7440203?hl=en) says this is a common status and does not necessarily indicate a problem. Getting every URL indexed is not the goal.

## Check the page, not just the totals

Pick one affected page that customers need. In [URL Inspection](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/9012289?hl=en), check:

- The current indexing status and last crawl date.
- Whether Google could fetch the page and indexing was allowed.
- The crawled content, when available, and any preferred page version Google reports.

Compare that information with the live page. A successful live test shows what Google can access now; it does not guarantee indexing. If the reports disagree, record their dates before assuming another problem appeared.

## Give the page a reason to exist

Imagine a contractor publishes twelve service pages that differ only in their city names. Before submitting them again, I would ask what a customer learns from each page that the others cannot answer.

A useful service page might explain the actual work offered, relevant limitations and what someone needs before requesting a quote. Adding another paragraph of repeated keywords does not answer those questions.

Google's [content assessment questions](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) focus on original information and useful detail. Use those questions to evaluate the page, without treating this status as proof that Google has diagnosed its content as poor.

## Make a focused change

Keep a short record: affected URL, inspection result, change made and date. Correct a confirmed issue or improve the page's usefulness, then monitor it. An indexing request cannot promise inclusion.

If Google cannot fetch the page, investigate [crawler access and CDN rules](/blog/cdn-googlebot-crawl-problems). If it is already indexed but displays an earlier title or description, that is a different question: [why Google still shows old website text](/blog/why-google-shows-old-website-text).
