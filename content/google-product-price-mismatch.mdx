If Google shows the wrong product price, compare the same variant and currency across Merchant Center and your website. One correct number on the page is not enough.

## Are you comparing the same item?

Open the exact product link submitted to Merchant Center. Check its selected colour, size, quantity and currency.

Imagine a blue bag costs CAD 40 and a red version costs CAD 45. Your Google listing describes the red bag, but its link opens the blue one. Start by checking the selected variant and where that link leads.

Google's [price requirements](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6324371?hl=en) cover the submitted amount, currency, landing page and checkout. Keep your comparison tied to one product ID.

## Which price is out of step?

I would collect these values together:

- The price and currency customers see for that variant.
- The current price in your product feed, the product information sent to Google.
- The price and currency in structured data, the page's machine-readable product details.
- Any sale price, sale dates and last update time.

Google's [mismatch guidance](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/14916353?hl=en) explains that a warning can reflect an earlier check, even when prices match now. Compare the reported timestamp with your website and feed updates.

This is a [connected website workflow](/blog/when-a-website-becomes-a-system): changing the shop price should also update the information your listing uses.

## Has Google processed the correction?

Correct the source of the mismatch, send current product data, and check the item's status in Merchant Center.

Google's [automatic item updates](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/12157888?hl=en) can correct some mismatches. They do not replace regular product updates or resolve every pricing problem.

Check the displayed price and the item's approval status separately. A corrected price does not guarantee approval, and the “Needs attention” status can take time to reflect a change.
