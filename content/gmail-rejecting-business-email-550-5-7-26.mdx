If Gmail rejects your business email with error 550 5.7.26, email authentication is one of the first things I would check. Your team might still receive messages normally while outgoing invoices or booking confirmations fail.

A delivery failure can be worrying when customers are waiting for a reply. The error code gives us a more useful starting point than simply saying the email is not working.

## What does the error mean?

Google checks whether a sending system is authorised to use a domain and whether the message passes the applicable authentication rules. Its [SMTP error guide](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/3726730?hl=en) lists several authentication failures that can produce 5.7.26.

You may see these terms in the message:

- SPF identifies permitted email senders for a domain.
- DKIM uses a signature to help recipients verify the message.
- DMARC tells receiving systems how to handle messages that fail aligned authentication checks.

Google's [sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126) explain that requirements differ for ordinary and bulk senders.

## Check every service that sends on your behalf

Imagine your staff use Google Workspace, but a separate website or invoicing platform sends confirmations from the same business domain. The staff emails may arrive while the automated messages are rejected.

I would identify the actual sending service and inspect the relevant authentication records. A website migration, new email provider or marketing platform can change what needs to be authorised.

## Be careful when changing DNS records

Adding an unrelated SPF record or removing an existing sender can disrupt email that already works. I would compare the current records with each legitimate provider's instructions before changing them. Correct authentication is important, but it does not guarantee inbox placement.

After a fix, test from each affected sending service, including your website if it sends notifications.
