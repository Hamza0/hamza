An address change that has not appeared on Google can mean different things. An edit may still be under review, may not have been approved, or may sit alongside a separate verification request. The exact message is the starting point.

I helped a client resolve a Google Business Profile address-change issue while they were losing business. Getting it resolved mattered to their daily operations. Their identity and the details of the resolution remain private, but the questions around the problem are useful to other business owners.

## What does the address-edit status mean?

Google reviews changes to verified profiles. Its [edit-status guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038311?hl=en) describes three outcomes:

| Status | What it tells you |
| --- | --- |
| Accepted | The updated information is displayed on the profile. |
| Pending | Google is still reviewing the edit. |
| Not approved | Google has not accepted the change, for example because it could not confirm its accuracy. |

Google says reviews usually take up to ten minutes, but can sometimes take up to thirty days. That describes its review window, not a promised resolution time for a particular business.

## Is a pending edit the same as verification?

An edit review concerns a requested information change. [Business verification](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242?hl=en) is a separate process for establishing control of the profile. Google determines which verification methods are available.

If one screen shows a pending address and another asks for verification, those messages should be recorded separately. Neither message, on its own, explains the whole history of the profile.

When the wording seems inconsistent, a useful account includes where each message appears and when it was seen. That gives a review something concrete to work from.

## Why does the business context matter?

Is the business moving, or is its existing address incorrect? Do customers visit the location, or does the business travel to them? Google's [business representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) distinguish the address and service-area information a business should provide.

For a customer planning a visit, unclear location information adds work: another phone call, another map search, or uncertainty about the journey. The practical concern is whether the public listing represents the business accurately.

My client's experience is one resolved case. Another profile needs its own assessment of the messages, business details and previous changes.
