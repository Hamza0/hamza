If customers see a privacy warning when opening your website, they may leave before they see your business. I would check the security certificate first, rather than asking visitors to ignore the warning.

A certificate warning means the browser cannot establish that the connection is trustworthy. An expired certificate, the wrong website address on a certificate or an incomplete hosting setup can all be involved.

## Check whether it happens to everyone

Open your website on another device and, if possible, a different network. Check both the version with www and the version without it. Make sure the date and time on the devices are correct.

If several devices show the same warning, I would investigate the website's HTTPS configuration. If only one device is affected, the cause may be local to that device. [Google's guide to Chrome errors](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/95669?hl=en) explains the different certificate messages.

## A recent website move can be a clue

Imagine a business moves its website to another hosting provider. The new pages are there, but the certificate for its domain has not been issued or configured correctly. Customers now see a warning even though the website content still exists.

I would check the domain, the certificate's validity, its covered names and the connection between the hosting provider and any proxy such as Cloudflare. Changing the website design will not address that problem.

## What to do next

Do not ask customers to bypass a certificate warning or enter payment information while the connection is untrusted. If you recently moved the website, tell me which hosting provider changed and when.

Send me the website address and the exact message you see. I can begin with the public connection and explain what I find before suggesting changes. If you are moving hosting, also consider [keeping your business email working](/blog/moving-website-keep-email-working).
