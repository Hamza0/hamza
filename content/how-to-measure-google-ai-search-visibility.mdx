---
title: "How Can I Measure My Website's Visibility in Google AI Search?"
publishedAt: "2026-11-24"
summary: "Use Search Console's generative AI impressions to understand visibility, compare equivalent periods and keep website enquiries separate from appearances in AI answers."
---

Use Search Console's Generative AI performance report to see how often links to your website appear in Google AI Overviews and AI Mode. Review the pages and audiences behind those impressions, then assess enquiries through your website's own reporting.

A visible link, a website visit and a useful enquiry describe different stages of discovery. Keeping those stages separate makes the numbers easier to interpret and gives you a clearer reason for the next website change.

## Start with the visibility report

Google [introduced the reports on June 3, 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/06/gen-ai-performance-reports) and states that worldwide rollout followed on August 31. The Search report provides impressions with breakdowns by page, country, device and date.

An impression records a link being shown in a supported generative AI feature. It does not establish that someone visited your website or contacted you. The report's documented metric is impressions; it does not provide a dedicated total of AI clicks or conversions.

If the report is missing, Google's [help documentation](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139) says the site may not have received enough impressions. It also points to the Search Console setting that controls inclusion in generative AI features. A missing report alone is not a diagnosis of poor content.

## Compare equivalent periods

Choose two complete periods of the same length, such as the last four complete weeks and the four before them. Keep the page, country and device filters consistent so the comparison answers the same question on both sides.

Record relevant changes, including a revised service page, a technical fix or a seasonal event. If several things changed at once, a rising graph cannot identify which change caused the increase.

Google notes that the newest report data can be preliminary. Give it time to settle before treating a short movement as a trend. Inspect individual pages as well as the overall total so one page's change does not hide another's.

## Connect each question to the right evidence

| Question | Evidence to review |
| --- | --- |
| Did links appear in Google AI answers? | Generative AI impressions |
| Did people visit the website? | Search performance and website analytics |
| Did visitors send an enquiry? | Confirmed form submissions or contact records |
| Were the enquiries relevant? | The requests received and subsequent conversations |

Avoid treating an increase in impressions as proof of more customers. These records do not, by themselves, connect a particular AI appearance to a particular enquiry. Review what your tracking can establish before making an attribution claim.

## Choose one useful improvement

If a relevant page receives visibility but visitors struggle to complete its contact path, inspect that journey. If the page's explanation leaves a recurring customer question unanswered, improve the explanation and record when you changed it.

My [note on clear answers and SEO](/blog/seo-and-aeo-start-with-clear-answers) covers the content foundations. Use the reporting to assess those decisions over time, while keeping the business goal specific: helping the right person understand your service and take an appropriate next step.
