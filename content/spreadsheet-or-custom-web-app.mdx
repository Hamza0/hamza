Keep your spreadsheet if sharing and updating it works reliably. I would consider an app when people need different access, clear approvals or stronger control over changes.

## Keep what already works

Several people editing at once is not, by itself, a reason to rebuild. Excel supports [shared editing and version history](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/excel/get-started/collaborate-on-excel-workbooks-at-the-same-time-with-co-authoring) when the file, storage location and applications meet its requirements.

A shared sheet can suit a trusted team tracking straightforward records. You need an agreed place to find the current file, clear column meanings and someone responsible for its structure.

Before commissioning software, ask what actually interrupts the work. If people struggle to interpret totals, [clearer labels and definitions](/blog/building-data-tools-for-non-technical-teams) may solve more than a new interface.

## Look at who can change what

Imagine a service team tracking jobs. Coordinators need to see every job, while contractors should see only their own assignments. Those access rules deserve more attention than the number of rows.

Locking cells can help prevent unwanted edits, but Microsoft says [worksheet protection is not a security feature](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/excel/protect-a-worksheet). It should not be treated as a way to keep confidential records hidden from someone receiving the workbook.

An app could provide separate views and permissions, but those controls must be built correctly. [OWASP's access-control guidance](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authorization_Cheat_Sheet.html) calls for checking permissions on every request. Hiding a button alone does not protect the underlying record.

## Follow one record through the team

Trace a job from entry to approval and completion. Who owns each stage? What happens when two people submit competing updates? Can someone tell whether a change was approved?

Decide what history you need: who changed a value, who approved it and when. A file's earlier versions and a record of business approvals answer different questions.

Compare an improved spreadsheet, an existing business tool and a custom app against those requirements. A custom build also needs maintenance, backups and an owner; those responsibilities do not disappear after launch.

I would start with [one manageable task](/blog/how-to-choose-the-first-workflow-to-automate) and prove the handoff works before moving everything.
