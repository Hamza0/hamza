SEO and answer engine optimization start with a useful answer. State the question your page addresses, answer it directly and support the explanation with verifiable details. Make the page accessible to search engines.

I use AEO to describe making content useful in answer-based search experiences. Google's current [generative AI optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) keeps established SEO practices at the foundation and emphasises distinctive, useful content. It also advises against unnecessary AI text files such as llms.txt for Google Search.

## Start with a question someone actually has

A service page should help someone make a decision. A technical note should explain a specific problem or approach. Choose the question from customer conversations or search data, identify the reader and decide what they need to understand by the end.

For example, someone asking why their website receives few enquiries needs a way to investigate. Explain how to review the offer, contact path and submission process before assuming the site needs more traffic.

Keep the scope narrow enough to provide a complete answer. Combine closely related questions when one useful page can cover them. Google's guide cautions against producing separate pages for every search variation primarily to influence rankings or AI responses.

## Put evidence beside the claim

Specific evidence gives readers something to assess. A case study can explain the problem, the work performed, what was measured and where the evidence stops. A technical recommendation can link to the relevant documentation.

| If the page says | Give the reader |
| --- | --- |
| A system became faster | The measurement method and its limits |
| A service solves a problem | The problem, scope and next step |
| A process is more reliable | The failure cases and checks involved |
| You have relevant experience | An accurate author profile and related work |

My [operations and financial systems case study](/projects/production-operations-platform) explains how its response times were measured and identifies the synthetic test data. Keeping the measurement method beside the result helps readers interpret it correctly.

Google's [helpful content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) similarly encourages clear sourcing, descriptive titles and information that helps the intended audience achieve its goal.

## Make the important information easy to find

Use headings that describe the sections, short paragraphs and links to relevant supporting pages. Put essential information in readable text. If you add structured data, it should reflect what visitors can actually see.

Check the technical foundations too. Google's AI guidance says a page must be indexed and eligible to appear in Search with a snippet. The site must also be included in Search generative AI features through its Search Console setting. These conditions establish eligibility; Google does not guarantee that an eligible page will appear.

## Review usefulness after publishing

Check which questions lead people to the page and whether those visitors find an appropriate next step. Review enquiry quality alongside traffic. If people keep asking a question the page was meant to answer, improve that explanation.

For Google AI features, [measure visibility using the appropriate Search Console report](/blog/how-to-measure-google-ai-search-visibility), then assess website visits and enquiries separately. The measurement note explains what the report can establish and how to make a useful comparison.

Update information when it changes and keep examples honest about their scope. Assess rankings and AI appearances using evidence, without promising either outcome.
