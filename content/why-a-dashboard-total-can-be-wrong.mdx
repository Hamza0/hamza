If your dashboard total looks wrong, I would check how the records were combined first. The original figures can be correct while the report counts an amount twice.

## Decide what you're counting

Are you measuring orders, invoices or payments received? Those are different things. Your reports may disagree because they use different dates, currencies, statuses or definitions.

I would write down what belongs in the total, then check whether the source holds current records, a history of changes or both. We need to establish that two figures measure the same thing before trying to make them agree.

## How $180 becomes $280

Imagine you have two orders. Order 101 totals $100 and has two items. Order 102 totals $80 and has one item. You expect an order total of $180.

To show product details, your report joins the orders and items tables. A *join* combines matching records:

| Order | Matching item | Order total |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| 101 | First item | $100 |
| 101 | Second item | $100 |
| 102 | First item | $80 |

Add the final column and you get $280. Your original orders were not duplicated. The report now has one row per item and repeats the order amount.

PostgreSQL's [join documentation](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/queries-table-expressions.html#QUERIES-JOIN) explains why each matching pair produces a row.

## Why removing duplicate amounts can fail

You might try `SUM(DISTINCT order_total)`. It appears to fix this example by counting $100 and $80 once each.

But [DISTINCT removes repeated values](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/sql-expressions.html#SYNTAX-AGGREGATES), not repeated orders. Give it two separate $100 orders and it returns $100 instead of $200.

I would make each included order contribute once, either by calculating from the orders table or summarising related records before combining them.

If you need product sales instead, we need different rules for item amounts, quantities, discounts, tax and refunds. That is a different calculation.

## Check a small sample you can explain

I would choose a short period and calculate its expected total by hand. Then I'd inspect the combined rows for repeated order identifiers.

Include orders with several items, separate orders with equal totals and missing related records. Compare the result with your dashboard under the same filters, and keep the example as a test for future changes.

Check missing values and update times too. A blank can mean missing information rather than zero. An import can finish successfully while supplying incomplete data.

Your screen should make those gaps visible, so you can judge whether the total is ready to use.
