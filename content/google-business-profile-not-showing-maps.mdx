Your profile may need verification, have a policy issue, or simply not rank for that search. I would first check whether customers can open the profile, then examine the exact search and location. Those are different problems to investigate.

## Find out whether the profile is public

Search for your business name and city in Maps. Open its share link as a visitor too, rather than relying only on the owner's management screen.

Then check the account that manages the profile. Record its verification status and any suspension or other warning. Google's [find-your-business guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/145585?hl=en) explains how verification, incomplete information and policy issues can affect visibility.

Keep the exact message. “I cannot find my business” gives less useful evidence than the profile link, search phrase, location and screenshot of what appeared.

## A public profile can still rank differently

Google describes local ranking through [relevance, distance and prominence](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en): how closely the business matches the search, how far away it is, and how well known it is.

For example, imagine a Barrie plumber whose profile appears for the business name but is absent from the first results for “plumber near me” searched in Toronto. That observation alone does not show the listing is disabled.

I would compare the actual search context before changing the profile. Google does not offer a way to request or pay for a better local ranking, and verification does not guarantee a particular position.

## Keep the information tied to the real business

Check the business name, main category, hours and location information against how the business actually operates. Google's [representation guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) cover businesses customers visit and businesses that travel to customers. Keep the information accurate and avoid duplicate profiles for the same business location.

Creating another listing just to become visible can introduce a second problem. First establish what happened to the existing one.

If visibility changed after an address edit, read my note on [Google Business Profile address problems](/blog/google-business-profile-address-change-help). That situation needs the edit status and verification context, rather than assumptions drawn from one Maps search.

If the profile has a suspension notice rather than a ranking or verification issue, see [what to check before appealing a suspended Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-suspended).
